## Clarification: balanced diagnostic versus natural proposal evaluation

The two commonly quoted C1 rows measure different populations and must not be
treated as duplicate estimates of the same score.

- **Balanced 256-box diagnostic:** 256 selected clean Test1 proposals, exactly 64
  examples per class across 34 scenes. It asks whether C1 can separate the four
  classes when every class has equal support. Macro-F1 was 0.553; radar P/R/F1
  was 0.694/0.531/0.602 and missile was 0.567/0.797/0.662.
- **Natural clean67 proposal evaluation:** every qualifying Stage1 proposal from
  67 held-out scenes, 4,231 proposals total. Gold proposal supports were radar 95,
  missile 195, other 2,920 and background 1,021. Radar had 54 TP, 221 FP and
  41 proposal-level FN; missile had 114 TP, 344 FP and 81 proposal-level FN.
  Therefore radar P/R/F1 was 0.196/0.568/0.292 and missile was
  0.249/0.585/0.349. Macro-F1 across all four classes was 0.410.

The natural row has much lower target precision because the detector routes
thousands of non-target candidates to the VLM. Its proposal-level recall is
conditional on candidates that reached C1. Including Stage1 target misses, the
end-to-end recall is lower: radar 54/152 = 0.355 and missile 114/235 = 0.485.

## Why diagnostic missile recall was higher

A read-only exact-ID comparison confirmed that the 64 diagnostic missile proposals
were not representative of all natural missile proposals.

| Cohort | Correct / support | Missile recall |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selected diagnostic IDs, original L4 run | 51/64 | 0.797 |
| Same 64 IDs in the full L40S run | 52/64 | 0.813 |
| Remaining clean Test1 missiles | 42/76 | 0.553 |
| All clean Test1 missiles | 94/140 | 0.671 |
| Clean Test2 missiles | 20/55 | 0.364 |
| Combined clean67 | 114/195 | 0.585 |

The diagnostic builder used deterministic random shuffling with seed 20261022 and
then capped each scene at four examples before filling the 64-per-class cohort.
This controlled scene concentration, but it also underweighted dense difficult
scenes. For example, scene `24APR01051503-S3DS-200012320748_01_P001` contributed
only four of its 23 missile proposals to the diagnostic; the full-run recall on
that scene was 1/23 = 0.043. Scene `24JAN04022720-S3DS-200013055297_01_P001`
was similarly difficult at 1/9.

The L4 versus L40S prediction agreement on the same 64 IDs was 61/64 = 95.3%, so
hardware/run variation is not the main explanation here. The evidence supports
scene- and appearance-dependent generalization failure plus an easier selected
diagnostic subset. It does not support treating 0.797 as the model's population
missile recall, nor does it prove the model learned no missile visual features.

`m0001_sep_w1_ensemble` result was recomputed with the saved comparison protocol:
confidence-descending, class-agnostic one-to-one matching at polygon IoU >= 0.50,
with exact raw class required for a correct classification.

| GT missiles | Correct missile | Wrong as vehicle | No matched box | Recall |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 23 | 8 | 15 | 0 | 8/23 = 0.348 |

Thus YOLO localized every missile GT in this scene under the common matching rule,
but its missile class recall was only 34.8% because 15 matched objects were labeled
`vehicle`. This is primarily a class-confusion failure, not a proposal/localization
miss for this scene.

## Historical B1 radar-to-missile error pattern

This offline analysis applies to the older `B1_a7` CoT checkpoint and the corrected
119-object Stage1-detected radar twin cohort. It is not a fresh C1 analysis.

| Paired outcome: GT box prediction -> detector-box prediction | Count |
|---|---:|
| radar -> radar | 44 |
| missile -> missile | 40 |
| radar -> missile | 21 |
| missile -> radar | 14 |

Detector-box radar recall was 58/119 = 48.7%; all 61 errors were radar -> missile.
Forty remained wrong even with the perfect GT box, while 21 were correct with the
GT box but changed to missile with the detector box. Thus classification is the
larger persistent problem, with a material but smaller box-sensitivity component.

The generated traces show a highly repeated learned rule:

| Cue in generated trace | Radar -> missile errors | Correct radar |
|---|---:|---:|
| Explicitly rejects radar because antenna/mast/turntable/raised element is absent | 61/61 | 13/58 |
| Describes an elongated object | 60/61 | 43/58 |
| Describes a combined flat/low and elongated signature | 56/61 | 29/58 |
| Mentions box/tarp/cover | 38/61 | 35/58 |
| Positively describes a canister-like/elongated load | 23/61 | 6/58 |

These are model-generated descriptions, not independent proof that the pixels truly
have those properties. In the analyst radar CSV, many hard-scene radars have an
antenna marked visible even though the model says none is visible. The evidence
therefore points to a perception-plus-shortcut failure: stowed, flat or small radar
systems resemble TELs at this resolution, and the learned discrimination text treats
failure to resolve an antenna as proof of missile.

Errors are also scene-concentrated in this 119-object detected subset:

| Scene | Radar support | Correct | Recall | Errors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `23JUN21043007-S3DS-200013457515_01_P001` | 18 | 8 | 44.4% | 10 |
| `22OCT24030421-S3DS-200011896352_01_P001` | 9 | 3 | 33.3% | 6 |
| `21NOV24030403-S3DS-200012895049_01_P001` | 8 | 2 | 25.0% | 6 |

The top three scenes contribute 22/61 errors. Earlier v2 analysis also found that
44% of missile training traces used absence of antenna/mast/turntable as a negative
radar rule, despite many training radars being stowed or box-like. Context sometimes
contains radar-site evidence, but the model does not reliably turn it into a
site-consistent radar decision.
