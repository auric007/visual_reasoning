## Output Order

The entire assistant response is one continuous target:

|Order|Section|Intended role|
|---|---|---|
|1|Planning|State what to inspect|
|2|Visual evidence|Describe candidate shape, structure, shadow, visible components|
|3|Grounding|Describe placement and surroundings|
|4|Discrimination|Compare possible classes|
|5|Synthesis|Summarize the decision|
|6|Answer|Output the class|
**These sections are learned text conventions.** We have no separate visual-evidence head, context head, or verification step checking each statement against the pixels. Either image can influence any section.

There is no extra weight for the final class, and no explicit penalty for a visually unsupported sentence beyond disagreement with the saved target text. A fluent rationale can therefore receive good training supervision even when its visual claims are questionable.

## Dataset balance and the important limitations

There were **1,960 training rows: 490 per class**. Radar had **290 distinct IDs**, repeated to reach 490; the other three classes each had 490 distinct IDs.

Radar/missile retained detailed analyst-reviewed explanations. Every `other` example used the same generic rejection explanation, and every `background` example used another fixed explanation.
rejection supervision had much less descriptive variety. Positive classes also contributed **77.3% of the corpus’s target-token occurrences**. That is a text-volume measurement; it does not automatically mean 77.3% of gradient weight

For representative samples, the close image supplied around **100–121 visual tokens**, while context supplied **960–1,089**. This confirms the token-count imbalance, but **token count alone cannot tell us which image controls the answer**.
